\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\textbf{Full-Duplex Spoken Dialog Models.}
Early work such as dGSLM~\citep{nguyen-etal-2023-generative} jointly modeled two conversational speech streams to capture turn-taking and overlap.
More recent end-to-end full-duplex models use either single-stream generation, as in SyncLLM~\citep{veluri-etal-2024-beyond}, OmniFlatten~\citep{zhang-etal-2025-omniflatten}, Raon-SpeechChat~\citep{kim2026raonspeechtechnicalreport}, and MiniCPM-o 4.5~\citep{cui2026minicpmo45realtimefullduplex}, or parallel user and assistant streams, as in Moshi~\citep{defossez2024moshispeechtextfoundationmodel} and PersonaPlex~\citep{11463413}.
Recent work further improves turn-taking, interruption handling, and response timing through interactivity training~\citep{pmlr-v267-wu25t,hsiao2026aspirinactionspaceprojection,ohashi2026multifacetedinteractivityalignmentfullduplex,li2026decouplingconversationaldynamicsfullduplex}.
Despite these advances, these models still advance the interaction autoregressively, requiring sequential backbone computation as new frames or chunks are generated.

\textbf{Accelerating Autoregressive Generation.}
Speculative and parallel decoding reduce sequential generation by predicting multiple future tokens and processing them with fewer target-model calls~\citep{NEURIPS2018_c4127b91,pmlr-v202-leviathan23a,chen2023acceleratinglargelanguagemodel}.
Methods such as Medusa~\citep{pmlr-v235-cai24b} and EAGLE~\citep{pmlr-v235-li24bt} improve drafting through additional prediction heads or feature-level prediction, and related approaches have been extended to autoregressive speech generation~\citep{10887855,10888194,11464026}.
PredGen~\citep{li2025predgen} and RelayS2S~\citep{mai2026relays2sdualpathspeculativegeneration} instead begin response generation before the complete spoken input is available, using partial user speech to prepare future output while subsequent input continues to arrive.
However, their speculative responses remain autoregressively generated.
They therefore move response computation earlier rather than reducing the sequential backbone steps required to predict multiple future interaction frames within an end-to-end full-duplex model.

\textbf{Diffusion-Based Parallel Generation.}
Masked and block diffusion replace token-by-token generation with parallel prediction over multiple positions~\citep{NEURIPS2021_958c5305,pmlr-v235-lou24a,NEURIPS2024_eb0b13cc,ICLR2025_7ede97c3}.
Recent work adapts pretrained autoregressive models to masked or block prediction~\citep{ICLR2025_0fa81c3f,cheng-etal-2026-sdar,ICLR2026_d0865cbe,gat2025setblockdecodinglanguage,samragh2025llmknowsfutureuncovering},
while DFlash (Chen et al., 2026a) and DiffuSpec (Li et al., 2026a) use diffusion models as parallel
drafters for autoregressive verification.
Rolling Diffusion Models~\citep{pmlr-v235-ruhe24a} and Diffusion Forcing~\citep{NEURIPS2024_2aee1c41} further consider causal or rolling prediction over temporal sequences.
Related ideas have recently been applied to speech: VocalNet-MDM~\citep{cheng2026vocalnetmdmacceleratingstreamingspeech} accelerates streaming half-duplex speech LLMs, while Chatterbox-Flash~\citep{seo2026chatterboxflashpriorcalibratedblockdiffusion} and DELTA-TTS~\citep{moon2026deltattsadaptingautoregressivemodel} adapt autoregressive TTS models for block or masked diffusion.
In these settings, farther output positions can be predicted from already available input.
Full-duplex dialog introduces a different constraint: future assistant frames depend on user speech that has not yet arrived.
DiffuPlex therefore predicts the missing future interaction jointly and revises only the unplayed assistant future as the corresponding user speech is observed.
